# Approved defaults and outstanding gates

The section-13 M0/M1 plan was approved; the user subsequently authorized M2 and continuation with M3 onward. Each milestone still uses a feature PR and requires explicit merge approval. These defaults resolve specification gaps, not external API guarantees.

## Live rating replaces voting (2026-10-16, issue #85)

The owner replaced the former voting phase (Borda ranking) with a live 0-10 rating per song during PLAYBACK. Phase order: `LOBBY -> GENRE_SPIN -> SUBMISSION -> PLAYBACK -> REVEAL -> (GENRE_SPIN | FINAL) -> LOBBY`. Defaults chosen:

- All scores, totals and deltas in state are integer tenths (`scoreTenths`); the song score is `round(sum*10/count)` (half up) and is shown with one decimal.
- `RATE {songId, value}` with the unchanged value is rejected `NOT_READY`; a song opens for rating at its start (preroll included) and stays open until PLAYBACK ends.
- Ratings of players who disconnect stay valid; eligible raters are the round participants who do not own the song.
- Owners get the song score as points; `linkedPoints: halfCeil` halves `ceil((score+bonus)/2)`. The `noRaters` bonus (fixed 5.0 per owner) is not halved and never wins the round bonus.
- A song with eligible raters but no rating scores 0.0 with the reveal note "no rating".
- A banger (at least one rating, all 10) gets a longer reveal slot (`BANGER_REVEAL_MS = 5000`).
- Old voting snapshots are not migrated: a running room from before this release is not supported (no compatibility shim, no ballot import).
- `RoundResult.reason` is `normal` or `noSongs`; `singleSong` is removed.
- The genre-fail flag is set once per song during PLAYBACK; the retract-until-finished behaviour of the old voting phase is gone.

## Engine guard decisions

- Retain `rouletteGenrePool` in GameState. The original single active-pool contract lost the injected catalogue on fixed/custom settings and could not switch back to roulette. The constructor's `genrePool` argument is the full source catalogue; active pool is derived from settings. No wire defaults or legacy shim.
- Fresh semantically unchanged commands are rejected with `NOT_READY`, preserve reference identity and are not cached. Only genuine accepted mutations enter the 256-command window; cached replays are accepted without another mutation. This avoids accepting an uncached command that could mutate state on a later replay. The adapter must use the pure classifier/cache, not version deltas alone.
- Nicknames remain reserved across every retained identity, including left/kicked records, so reconnect can preserve the original name. Rematch may release names of pruned left identities; kicked identities/names remain until a new roomId.
- Quorum is connected current-round participants, not late-join spectators; the ten-client room cap includes spectators. Host election prefers status player, then spectator; replica ACK selection is role-agnostic. All clientId ties are code-unit lexical, not locale/ICU collation.
- Exact fractional clipSeconds are retained; playback durations use `max(1, round(clipSeconds * 1000))` integer milliseconds for deadlineAt. Positive clips always advance time, with at most millisecond quantization rather than changing chosen media metadata.
- A round where raters exist but nobody rated has every song at 0.0, so every eligible song receives the approved +2 tie bonus. A positive score threshold would be a separate product change.
- Ten admitted active identities is a hard cap even on reconnect. Already admitted present bindings are never evicted automatically; an active identity may rebind a tab without another slot. Inactive JOIN returns `ROOM_FULL` while full; Presence removes absent bindings first and recovers retained inactive bindings in joinedAt/code-unit order only into free slots. Overflow stays disconnected with score/drafts/identity retained until capacity frees. Left/kicked identities are not auto-restored by Presence.
- UI write hints and rating/flag target lists require the local `viewerConnectionId`, not just clientId. Stale tabs remain read-only after a real rebind while their own stored choices stay readable. A bound player can change a rating until PLAYBACK ends; a genre-fail flag is set once.

## M2 integration decisions

- `HOST_CHANGED` requires full Presence connection IDs and applies to the unmodified adopted snapshot. A separate Presence mutation follows under the new host; stale connected flags do not alter the chosen migration base.
- `HOST_HANDOFF` explicitly moves the current host's write connection to its latest accepted tab at the same epoch. The old confirmed connection announces the new binding; peers validate against the old binding, then accept ordinary snapshots from the new one. No fake sender or replica alias.
- Reconnect discovers a retained nickname from the complete live snapshot; no local nickname/game persistence. An unknown identity must enter a nickname.
- `ViewerPermissions.canWrite` exposes the bound tab's phase-independent write permission for Sound and read-only lobby controls. The read-only hint describes the authorization requirement without assuming another tab is the cause; removed identities also remain read-only, with their status visible in the roster.
- Five-second snapshot inactivity timeout was explicitly approved to keep the full 128-part/6-MiB codec bound compatible with fanout-aware pacing. This does not change AK-4/5 or promise a project-wide quota guarantee. See ADR-002.
- M2 Sound consent unlocks Web Audio only. Real YouTube preview validation/autoplay is M3/M4; the lobby does not expose a misleading game-start control before gameplay screens exist.

## M4 integration decisions and runtime gates

- Reuse existing snapshot playback timestamps and session clock synchronization; no new protocol event, reducer transition or network ticker. A privacy-safe playback selector is the only engine addition.
- Embedding consent (#96) is one stored choice (`banger-roulette.youtubeConsent`), collected by the start gate and shared by submission, playback and reveal; the drawer revokes it. "Ohne Videos" is not stored and also unlocks audio, so the player can still be ready to start. After a reload only the one-tap sound gate appears (browsers need a gesture per page load). Local volume defaults to 70%, with mute/volume retained across songs for the controller's lifetime (song selection through playback of one round).
- CUED gates scheduled play. Late cue or buffering catches up only on actual PLAYING; errors skip locally and never extend authoritative deadlines. Seeking above 750 ms drift uses a one-second cooldown and an independent deadline because YouTube seeks cancel `endSeconds`.
- Real unmocked YouTube/oEmbed was exercised in four isolated UI/session/engine contexts over the test transport: three consented players plus one nonconsenting listener. First PLAYING for the official example video arrived 3608/4570/4723 ms after the common planned start; later drift samples were -508/-434/-309 ms. AK-7's real cold-start ±500 ms criterion was not met. This run predates two later player fixes (embed-load CUED race, pre-start PLAYING) and is not a live Supabase timing claim; the debug overlay retains the failed start measurements.
- After the pre-start PLAYING fix, one controller with the API already loaded played the same video against real YouTube with a 15-second preroll. First PLAYING arrived 619 ms after `startAt`. Drift stayed between -561 and -625 ms, below the 750 ms correction threshold, and the independent deadline ended the clip. This single warm-player sample also misses ±500 ms; it is not a multi-client acceptance result.
- After both player fixes, three consented clients played two real songs over the test transport (official example video and Big Buck Bunny, 20-second clips). First PLAYING lagged `startAt` by 2851/3928/3377 ms on the cold first song and 1952/2155/2069 ms on the second. After drift correction all three stayed within ±500 ms of the shared timeline (-474/-380/-380 and -353/-446/-374 ms), about 94 ms apart. Cueing loads only the thumbnail; the stream request starts with `playVideo`, so AK-7's start criterion is not met without pre-start buffering. The real preview also passed at a 360px viewport.
- User decisions after these measurements: (1) muted pre-start buffering is approved; the invariant "never play before `startAt`" becomes "never audible and never claimed before `startAt`". (2) The playback player is pre-warmed after consent during song selection instead of lengthening the first preroll.
- Two further designs were measured against real YouTube and rejected: a learned early-resume lead unmuted exactly at `startAt` but left content offsets up to ±650 ms between clients, and silent seek alignment before `startAt` caused real rebuffering with 2–2.5 s outliers. The final design pauses the muted buffer at `startSeconds` and restarts 250 ms before `startAt` while muted, or keeps it running muted if under 500 ms remain. Best warm results in this rig were 55–102 ms audible start with about 40 ms spread between clients.
- AK-7 is not proven on real YouTube. All local multi-client runs put three headless Chromium clients on one 2-vCPU VM that also software-decodes the streams (including a 4K60 video). Repeated runs of the same build varied from 55 ms to over 1.5 s on the same videos, so this rig cannot separate code from host contention. Acceptance needs one client per device on the Preview deployment once Supabase Presence works again.
- The e2e YouTube fixture advances media time with `performance.now()` and caps it at the video duration. M3 start tests therefore assert deterministic user choices (`t=10` plus +5 restored as 15) and the last-whole-second boundary (199 for a 200-second video), not an incidental sampled playhead.
- The production-entry attempt still created a room but peer JOIN returned ROOM_NOT_FOUND (2/2). Redacted frame capture: the host's Presence track is followed by `phx_error` `channel_crash` and a rejoin; the guest's STATE_REQUEST reaches the host and the host's snapshot reaches the guest, which correctly rejects it because discovery requires the broadcaster to be a live Presence tuple. A standalone SDK probe on a fresh random topic reproduced it independent of app payloads: a minimal `track({a:1})` timed out and the channel failed with `transport failure` about every five seconds, while a broadcast-only channel stayed joined and delivered. This matches Supabase's open [Realtime latency incident](https://status.supabase.com/incidents/01M4S5FT4GCZ8BWV2W1RB5WXNE) (Presence instability). Bypassing Presence would replace the specified liveness/roster/migration mechanism with broadcast heartbeats, an architecture change against SPEC section 3 and NFR-02 that needs an ADR and approval. Live multiplayer acceptance remains blocked; no M2 guards or AK targets were weakened.

## M5 integration decisions (historical; superseded 2026-10-16 by live rating, issue #85)

- No drag-and-drop library (bundle budget NFR-01): native HTML5 drag-and-drop for desktop, tap-to-append for touch, up/down buttons for keyboard. Each discrete change sends the complete ranking; the host rate limit (burst 20, 10/s) is never approached by a human.
- Reveal order is "ascending placement": round winners always come last, other songs by ascending Borda. A genre-failed song with the highest Borda is revealed before the winner instead of looking like one.
- Advancing out of VOTING and REVEAL stays deadline-driven or automatic (everyone finished). No host "skip" button was added; the engine's host `ADVANCE` remains available to tests.
- The voting re-listen player and voting/reveal thumbnails reuse the shared embedding consent; without consent nothing is loaded from YouTube.
- A quorum or host pause during VOTING empties the write-target lists. For voters who have not finished, the voting view now keeps the foreign songs visible read-only with a pause notice instead of "no foreign songs to rank"; finished voters keep their read-only ballot.

## Live multiplayer evidence (2026-10-14, after the Supabase Realtime incident)

- Supabase reported the Realtime latency incident as "monitoring". A standalone Presence probe on fresh topics succeeded 10/10 with 0 channel errors; the peer saw the tracked tuple 0.3–3.2 s after subscribing.
- Four headless Chromium contexts played the public production deployment (main `7c953c4`, real Supabase, Vercel API, YouTube nocookie), 2 rounds, driven only through the UI. 4/4 full runs passed: AK-1 identical genre on all clients; AK-2 linked song shown once, both owners scored, neither could rank or flag it; AK-3 no owner names before REVEAL; AK-4 new host after 877/646/2721 ms with round data intact; AK-5 reload back in the room after 1515/2228/2368 ms with an unchanged score. Zero CSP violations and zero application console errors.
- AK-7 remains unproven: all four clients ran on one VM (start skew −461…+1262 ms, max drift 175–620 ms). Acceptance still needs one client per device.
- Decision (issue #15): one automatic internal retry (`TRANSIENT_RETRY_LIMIT = 1`) covers exactly that first-attempt flake, with no UI change. `createRoom` reruns the whole create once with a fresh transport and a new room code when an attempt throws (never after the `MAX_CREATE_ATTEMPTS` collision loop is exhausted); `joinRoom` reruns the whole join once on `ROOM_NOT_FOUND` or a thrown transport/presence error; status stays `connecting` throughout. `INVALID_ROOM_CODE`, `INVALID`, `ROOM_FULL`, rejected JOIN results, stale generations and a disposed session are never retried, and a newer create/join cancels a pending retry. Discovery guards (live Presence tuple) and the 4 s discovery timeout are unchanged, so a genuinely missing room now reports `ROOM_NOT_FOUND` after two discovery windows (about 8 s).

## Pixel-Filz redesign decisions (#45)

- Spin token shortened: `dur-spin` goes from 5200 ms to 2580 ms. The spin plus the 420 ms shake fits `SPIN_MS = 3000`, so the engine stays unchanged. Superseded by #94 (genre reel): the result vanished the moment the shake ended, so `SPIN_MS` is now 5500 ms and `dur-spin` 3200 ms: the reel spins about 3200 ms with deceleration, then the result stays at least 2000 ms (plate pop-in, light sweep, gold window; 2300 ms with the current numbers) before SUBMISSION, where the genre stays as a plate at the top. The motion is a cubic ease-out of the drum angle (the old bezier with its flattened launch is gone), still a pure function of server-synced time, so every client stops on the same genre. The tick sound now follows the rows that pass the window and the stop sound plays at the stop instead of at the phase change.
- Jackpot: `dur-jackpot` of 2400 ms fits `REVEAL_PER_SONG_MS = 2500`, unchanged.
- THIS IS A BANGER show (#87): `BangerShow` runs the whole `BANGER_REVEAL_MS = 5000` slot of a 10.0 song at 120 bpm (stamp lands at 300 ms, one beat per 500 ms, finale at 3800 ms) and replaces the jackpot show for that entry; the winner badge stays. The strobe flashes twice a second at most (WCAG 2.3.1) and is gone under reduced motion, which keeps the slogan, the card and a static gold burst as fades. ## Spiritually Linked for different uploads of one song (#106)

- Decision: FR-26 no longer requires identical video ids. `src/engine/songMatch.ts` (pure, no I/O, no network) groups submissions at the end of SUBMISSION, exactly where identical-id grouping ran before, with union-find over real pairwise matches. Two uploads are the same song when the ids are equal, or when all guards hold: both channels belong to the artist (channel guard below), duration within `max(4 s, 3 % of the shorter)` (unknown or 0 never links), identical version-marker text, identical unknown-title-part text, equal track name, equal artist token sets (see the artist bullet; #115 removed the former token Jaccard >= 0.5 rule). There is no fuzzy threshold on the track name, so "Hello" never matches "Hello Adele". Strictness over recall: every guard resolves doubt to "not linked".
- Contract: `Song` gains `memberVideoIds` (canonical id first, then the other uploads). The canonical id is the first accepted submission's `videoId`; RATE and FLAG keep targeting it, and owners are all members' submitters. `GameStateSchema` rejects duplicate member ids across songs, a canonical id that is not first, and owners that do not match the member submissions. Snapshots from a host that predates #106 omit the field: `SongSchema` fills a missing `memberVideoIds` with `[song.media.videoId]` at ingress (round songs and `results[*].songs[*].song`); a present but invalid array is still rejected. `protocolVersion` stays 1. SPEC FR-26 is updated to the widened rule.
- Research (checked 2026-10-17): the track-matching code of playlist migration tools matches on title plus artist plus duration; `abdulmuhg/harmonize` accepts a fuzzy score of title x 0.6 + artist x 0.4 >= 0.7 with a +-5 s duration check, and `lystapp/open-music-normalize` (listed in search, README not fetchable) separates title, features and version tags before comparing. Fuzzy title similarity is rejected here on purpose (a similarity threshold merges "Shape of You" and "Shape of Water"). Taken over: split off featuring clauses, treat version tags as a separate key, a duration window.
- Noise words (dropped only from metadata segments, never from the artist/track pair): official, oficial, music, video, videoclip, clip, audio, lyric(s), lyrical, lyric video, paroles, letra, testo, visualizer, hd, hq, uhd, 4k, 8k, 1080p, 720p, mv, m/v, explicit, clean, stereo, out now, premiere, full song/audio/video, with lyrics, high quality, song, topic, vevo, new, plus remaster tags with an optional year and "original mix". Parse the artist/track pair before dropping trailing dash metadata: "Adele - Song" keeps "Song", and "Adele - Song - Official Audio" drops only the last segment. A track consisting only of noise words is still a real track name. Existing bare suffix handling (for example "Yellow Lyrics") remains; an empty track or one reduced to the artist name by dropping a suffix, credit, pipe or bracket segment is uncertain and never links different video ids. That decision is taken against the raw title, before any of this text is dropped, so it also covers an explicit artist/track pair: "Adele - Adele | Song", "Adele - Adele (Song)" and "Adele - Adele [Song]" are not the track "Adele" and stay apart from it and from each other, while the untouched "Adele - Adele" still links the bare "Adele". An explicit missing track never falls back to the artist as its track.
- Version markers (must be identical on both sides, otherwise not linked): live, remix, rmx, cover, acoustic, unplugged, slowed, reverb, sped up, speed up, nightcore, instrumental, karaoke, 8d, bass boosted, extended, radio edit, edit, demo, mashup, mix, version (covers "Taylor's Version"), ver, rework, bootleg, vip, flip, piano, orchestral, a cappella, acapella, re-recorded, and beyond the issue list: dub, stripped, session(s), reprise, part N, skit, interlude, intro, outro, alternate, alternative, lofi, chopped, screwed, megamix, tribute. The additions only ever make linking stricter. A marker segment is compared as text, so "(Live at Wembley)" differs from "(Live at Knebworth)". Channel names count only for unambiguous markers (remix, cover, karaoke, nightcore, instrumental, piano, acoustic, slowed, reverb, 8d, bass boosted, sped up, mashup, bootleg, rework, orchestral); "live" or "intro" in a channel name is ordinary.
- Artist: from "Artist - Track" when the title has exactly one separator after dropping noise and marker parts, otherwise from a Topic or VEVO channel only (see the channel guard). Artists match only when their normalized token sets are equal (#115, replaces the token Jaccard >= 0.5 rule of #106): order and the stop words "and" and "x" are ignored, so "Calvin Harris & Dua Lipa" still links "Dua Lipa & Calvin Harris", while "Pink" never links "Pink Floyd" and a credit that names a featured artist on one upload only ("Calvin Harris & Dua Lipa" against "Calvin Harris", "Pink & Nate" against "Pink") stays apart. There is no space-insensitive comparison, so "AB" and "A B" are different artists. Unknown artist on either side never links.
- Unknown title parts: only the noise words above (and remaster tags) are dropped from a bracket, a pipe segment or a dash part. Any other bracket, pipe or subtitle text stays in the comparison key and must be identical on both uploads, so "Hello (Goodbye)" and "Hello (Again)", or "Intro [Album One]" and "Intro [Album Two]", stay two songs. A credit after ft./feat./featuring is cut, but its tail is read for version markers first ("Get Lucky ft. Pharrell Williams live" never links to the studio cut).
- Channel guard (PM decision of 2026-10-17, replaces the earlier Jaccard guard; different songs must never be merged, recall may drop): two different uploads link only if BOTH uploading channels belong to their artist. The channel name loses " - Topic", a trailing "VEVO" (CamelCase names such as "LionelRichieVEVO" are split into words first) and the trailing words Official, Music and Records, nothing else; the remaining token set C must be non-empty and a SUBSET of the artist token set A (the title's artist split on whitespace, "&", "x", "and", commas and feat./ft. credits, so a credited featured artist counts as part of A). There is no Jaccard in the channel check. "Adele", "Adele - Topic", "AdeleVEVO" and "Adele Records" belong to Adele; "Calvin Harris" belongs to "Calvin Harris & Dua Lipa"; "Adele Fan", "Adele Lyrics", "Sing King" and "7clouds" do not. Denylist: a channel whose normalized name (also CamelCase-split) contains fan, fans, lyric, lyrics, karaoke, cover, covers, tribute, nightcore, slowed, sped or 8d, or the names "sing king", "7clouds" or "lyrical lemon", never links a different upload; an identical videoId stays linked. Artist fallback: a title without "Artist - Track" takes the artist from the channel ONLY for " - Topic" and VEVO channels; any other channel without an artist in the title leaves the artist unknown, so it never links ("Hello" and "Hello (Audio)" on Sing King stay two songs). The artists of the two uploads still have to match (equal artist token sets, see the artist bullet).
- Known false negatives (accepted, pinned by tests): a title in another script than its Topic upload, a track subtitle only one upload carries, an artist channel named differently from the credit ("The Weeknd - Topic" against the title credit "Weeknd", a label or management channel, a channel in another script), an official upload from a channel that is neither the artist nor Topic/VEVO, a title without an artist on such a channel, a credit written "with X" ("Get Lucky with Pharrell Williams" stays apart from "Get Lucky"), an unbracketed remaster suffix ("Bohemian Rhapsody Remastered 2011" stays apart from "Bohemian Rhapsody"), a credit tail with a marker against the same marker in brackets, an artist whose channel is a differently spelled CamelCase name, an artist credit that names a featured artist on one upload only ("Pink & Nate" against "Pink", "Calvin Harris & Dua Lipa" against "Calvin Harris"), and a title whose only track content sits in a pipe or bracket segment that noise stripping removes: "Adele | Song" and "Adele (Song)" never link "Adele" or "Adele - Adele", and because the segment is not read as the track they also stay apart from "Adele - Song", and a self-titled track whose title carries any dropped segment ("Adele - Adele (Official Audio)", "Adele - Adele | Song") stays apart from a bare "Adele" upload, and a channel whose whole name is a dropped suffix word ("Music - Topic", "Records - Topic") leaves the artist unknown, so "Music" on "Music - Topic" never links a different upload. Real titles are never shortened ("Stuck With Me" keeps "With"). They stay two songs.
- Residual false-positive risk (open, no absolute safety claimed): two uploads by the artist's own channels with the same artist, the same bare track name and a duration inside the window still link, even if they are different recordings on different albums that carry no marker or subtitle (for example two studio tracks both titled "Intro" with matching length). The rule has no access to album, ISRC or audio data. A stricter rule (for example requiring a shared album or a manual confirmation) is the owner's call.
